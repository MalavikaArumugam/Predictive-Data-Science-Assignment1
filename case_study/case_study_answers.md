# Case Study: Predicting Patient Re-admission in Healthcare

## (a) Identify the problem faced by the hospital. (2 Marks)
Johns Hopkins Hospital faces frequent patient readmissions within 30 days of discharge. These readmissions can increase healthcare costs, put additional pressure on hospital resources, and indicate that some patients may need more support after leaving the hospital. The hospital therefore wants to identify patients who are at higher risk of returning so that preventive care can be arranged.

## (b) Explain the input data used for building the predictive model. (3 Marks)
The case study mentions Electronic Health Record (EHR) data, including **patient medical history, laboratory results, and hospital stay details**. Medical history may describe previous illnesses and chronic conditions; laboratory results provide measurements about the patient's condition; and hospital stay details describe the current admission and discharge. Data should be checked for errors and missing values, and patient information must be handled securely.

## (c) Describe how predictive analytics helps identify high-risk patients. (4 Marks)
Predictive analytics uses patterns in historical records to estimate the likelihood of future readmission. The workflow includes collecting and preparing EHR data, selecting suitable features, training a model on past records with known outcomes, evaluating it on separate data, and applying it to a newly discharged patient's information. The result is a risk estimate that can flag patients for follow-up. It is not a guarantee and does not replace clinical judgment.

## (d) Discuss the actions taken and the benefits of using predictive analytics in healthcare. (5 Marks)
For higher-risk patients, providers may arrange earlier follow-up appointments, explain discharge instructions clearly, review medication plans, coordinate with primary-care teams, and call patients after discharge. Potential benefits include earlier intervention, improved continuity of care, better prioritization of staff time, more targeted use of resources, and a possible reduction in avoidable readmissions and costs. Model performance, fairness, privacy, and the effect of interventions should be monitored.

## (f) Analyze how the predicted risk scores help healthcare providers make decisions. (5 Marks)
A predicted risk score summarizes the model's estimate of a patient's likelihood of readmission in the defined period. Providers may use it to prioritize follow-up calls, schedule earlier reviews, coordinate services, and identify patients who may need extra discharge support. Action thresholds should reflect available resources and the consequences of missing a high-risk patient. Scores are estimates and should be combined with clinical judgment, patient preferences, and checks for errors or bias.
